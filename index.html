The mission

Deep space · 2027

We do not cross the void because it is quiet. We cross it because the horizon keeps moving — and something in us refuses to let it go first.

Built for distance

Ardent-1 is a crew vehicle engineered for 107-day sorties beyond low Earth orbit: closed-loop life support, redundant propulsion, and a hull that shrugs off the radiation belts so the crew doesn't have to.

Flown by people

Automation handles the paperwork. Every critical burn, docking and entry is confirmed by four humans who have trained together for six years and can finish each other's checklists.

Flight plan

T-minus to splashdown
  1. T−00:00:07

    Ignition

    Nine engines climb to full power. The hold-down arms bear 7.6 million newtons before they let go.

    Phase A · Ground
  2. T+00:00:00

    Liftoff

    Ardent-1 clears the tower at Kennedy LC-39B and rolls ninety degrees onto its ascent azimuth.

    Phase A · Ascent
  3. T+00:08:44

    Stage separation

    The core stage falls away over the Atlantic; the upper stage lights and carries the crew the rest of the way up.

    Phase B · Ascent
  4. T+00:42:15

    Orbit insertion

    185 × 235 km parking orbit. Every system reads green, and the crew unbuckles for the first time.

    Phase B · Earth orbit
  5. Day 03

    Trans-lunar injection

    A five-minute burn commits Ardent-1 to the Moon. Earth shrinks to a coin behind them.

    Phase C · Coast
  6. Day 06 → 21

    Lunar operations

    Six EVAs in the Aitken Basin: 1.4 tonnes of samples, one deployable relay, no shortcuts.

    Phase D · Surface
  7. Day 107

    Entry & splashdown

    Direct entry at 11 km/s, four parachutes, and the Pacific under a bright morning sun.

    Phase E · Return
